First decision

How many nights do you need?

Three or four. Everything below is written for three nights — Friday to Monday — because that is what most groups end up choosing.

Two nights

Too short

An expensive way to spend most of your time in a van. One dinner, one hangover, and you're back at the airport.

Three nights

The sweet spot

A welcome dinner, a day on the water and one night out, with a slow day in the middle. This is the plan below.

Four nights

If you're flying far

Buys you a second slow day, or a day in the other town. Worth it if the flight is long or the group is large.

Five or more

The group splits

Past five nights people start making their own plans. Nothing wrong with that — just don't plan the whole week together.

Cheapest time to come: September and early October, when the town is at its emptiest. It is also hurricane season, and we would rather say so here than let you find out in August. From November the town fills up and does not empty again until after Easter. December 31 and the week around it book out months ahead.

Sunday

The slow day
09:30

Breakfast at the villa, no alarm

Coffee, fruit, eggs, gallon water and Gatorade are all in the pre-stock for exactly this morning.

11:30

Pick one beach, not three

Chileno Bay and Santa María Bay, both on the corridor between the two towns, are the sheltered ones people actually swim and snorkel in; Santa María is the barer of the two, so take shade, water and anything else you'll want. Playa El Médano in Cabo San Lucas is the one with bars and service attached. Playa Palmilla sits on the San José side. Costa Azul, further up, is where the surf lessons are. Not every stretch of this coast is swimmable — the open-Pacific side, including the beach beside the Arch, is not, and it is worth asking locally before anyone goes in.

14:00

17:30

Sunset

Sunset is a Pacific-side event, which means Land's End, the marina or anywhere facing west. Sunset cruises run out of the Cabo San Lucas marina by the handful — operators, boats and prices vary enough that we'll quote you one rather than list them.

Logistics

Getting around Los Cabos.

The two towns are about 20 miles apart along one highway. Almost every bad hour of a Cabo bachelorette happens in that gap.

01

Book the airport run

Pre-booked and paid, round trip, one vehicle for up to 11 with luggage. Don't build the arrival around ride-hailing — availability in Los Cabos has been inconsistent, and airport pickup in particular is not something to count on.

02

Taxis aren't metered

Agree the fare out loud before anyone gets in, and expect it to be more than it looks. For a group of ten, one booked vehicle beats three taxis on both price and patience.

03

Between the towns

Roughly 30–40 minutes each way on the corridor highway, longer at rush hour or on a cruise-ship day. Plan one crossing per day, not three.

04

What is walkable

The Cabo San Lucas marina and downtown, and San José's historic centre, are both easy on foot. Everything between them is not. Flat shoes for San José's cobblestones.

Tipping is customary in Mexico and the usual range people work to is 15–20%; gratuity is not included in any of our package prices, so bring small bills. We'd also rather you drank bottled or filtered water all weekend — the villa pre-stock includes gallon and bottled water for that reason.

Sunday, honestly

The hangover day.

Every real itinerary has one and most published ones pretend it doesn't exist. Plan it on purpose and it becomes the day people talk about.

Protect the morning. Nothing before 11:00, and nobody has to explain themselves. The one thing worth doing early is drinking water, which is why the pre-stock carries gallon water, bottled water, Gatorade, coconut water and fresh orange juice rather than only tequila.

One activity, maximum, and it should be flat. A sheltered bay — Chileno or Santa María — where the water is calm and you can lie in it. Not a second boat day. Not a 4x4 tour booked at 02:00 by whoever was still awake.

Let the food come to you. This is the day a chef in the villa earns the fee twice over: a late brunch or an early long dinner with nobody driving, nobody queueing and nobody chasing a bill for ten. Add it in the builder and the per-guest number barely moves — $600 across ten people is $60 each before tax.

Massages, if you're going to do them, go here. Sunday afternoon, by the pool, is the slot that works. Our in-home spa service is priced on its own site.

And leave the night loose. If the group wants a second night out, fine — but book the ride, not the table.

Before you fly

What to actually pack.

Food, drink, decor and the bar are already handled. This is the rest of it.

01

For the water

Two swimsuits per pool day, a cover-up you can sit down to lunch in, reef-safe sunscreen for the protected bays, a hat, and motion-sickness tablets for whoever needs them on the boat.

02

For the nights

One outfit you'd be happy to be photographed in, and shoes you can stand in for four hours. Between November and March the evenings get cool enough to want a layer.

03

For the town

Flat shoes — San José's centre is cobblestone. Small bills in cash for tips, which are customary and not included in our prices.

04

Admin

Passports and whatever the current entry rules ask of your group — check them close to the date rather than trusting a blog. Travel insurance if your dates fall in hurricane season.

What you can leave at home: snacks, mixers, party cups, bagged ice, sunscreen for the villa, and anything decorative. The grocery pre-stock covers the first four and the welcome decor covers the last — both itemised on the packages page. Bring the personal things: the sashes with names on them, the photos, the playlist.

About this plan

The questions it raises.

Straight answers. The pricing questions are answered on the packages page.

Can we run this itinerary on a Thursday-to-Sunday instead?

Yes, and the shape holds: arrival night in the villa, the water day in the middle, the night out the evening before the slow day. The only thing that genuinely moves is the Art Walk in San José's Gallery District, which is a Thursday-evening, high-season thing — if your Thursday is the arrival day, that is a reason to land early rather than a reason to skip the welcome dinner.

How far ahead do we have to book the services in this plan?

Less far than you'd think. We have put a chef in a villa with eight hours' notice. What genuinely needs months is the handful of dates the whole town wants at once — December 31 above all, and the week around it. If your weekend falls there, write now, not in November.

Do we need the yacht day?

No, and it is the single biggest line in the weekend at $1,600, so it is the one worth deciding honestly. It is what the Saturday is built around and what most groups say they came for. If it goes, replace it with a long beach day at a sheltered bay and move the chef dinner to Saturday night — the builder will show you exactly what the split drops to.

What is in this plan that you don't run yourselves?

The villa, the flights, the restaurants, the clubs, the beaches and the sunset cruises. We run the chef, the barman, the groceries, the decor, the yacht charter and the vehicles — those are the lines you'll see priced on this page. Everything else we'll happily book, ask about or drive you to, but we are not going to publish someone else's prices or hours as if they were ours.

Villa or hotel, really?

Villa, for a group. But check the community's rules before you sign: a few — Montecristo and Novaispania among them — require you to use their own chef and will not let ours in. Send us the address and we'll tell you what it allows.
